What this release can—and cannot—say

TrabahoLens shows what is actually known about Philippine occupations, and what is not. Every figure carries the kind of evidence it is, the source it came from, the period it refers to, and the level at which that source published it. Where no evidence exists, the metric says so instead of showing a number.

Loading release status… What is implemented, real-source verified, fixture-tested, or blocked differs per pipeline stage; the tables below preserve those distinctions. The remaining gaps are recorded per source and in docs/limitations.md.

The previous version of this tool displayed employment, wage, informality, overseas-worker and hiring figures that were not measurements. They have been withdrawn rather than relabelled. See the baseline audit for what each one actually was.

Generative-AI task exposure

Employment outcomes also depend on adoption, costs, regulation, demand, productivity, how work gets reorganised, worker adaptation, institutional capacity, and the creation of new work. None of those is measured here.

How a figure was produced

The derivation class says how a number came to exist. Hover or focus a badge for the plain-language explanation. Note that a sample survey is an estimate even when an official agency publishes it — most official labour statistics are.

Who produced it

The source authority says who stands behind a number. It is a separate question from how the number was produced, and the two are shown separately so neither is mistaken for the other.
